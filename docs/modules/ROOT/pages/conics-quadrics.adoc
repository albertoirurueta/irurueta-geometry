= Conics and Quadrics

NOTE: This documentation was generated with the assistance of AI. Please report any inaccuracies.

Conics are second-degree curves in the 2D projective plane; quadrics are their 3D analogue, second-degree
surfaces. Both come with a **dual** representation (tangent lines / tangent planes instead of points) and both
have familiar specializations -- circle/ellipse for conics, sphere/ellipsoid for quadrics.

== Conics

image::conic-sections.svg[Conic sections obtained by cutting a double cone with a plane, classified by the sign of b squared minus a c]

`BaseConic` is an abstract 3&#215;3 symmetric-matrix base class for both `Conic` and `DualConic`. A conic is the
locus stem:[ax^2 + 2bxy + cy^2 + 2dxw + 2eyw + fw^2 = 0]; its matrix form is:

[cols="^,^,^"]
|===
|a |b |d

|b |c |e

|d |e |f
|===

`Conic.isLocus(point)` tests stem:[\mathbf{p}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{C}\, \mathbf{p} \approx 0]. `Conic.getConicType()` classifies the
conic from the sign of stem:[b^2 - ac]:

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|`ConicType` |Condition

|`CIRCLE_CONIC_TYPE`
|stem:[b^2-ac<0], and additionally stem:[a=c], stem:[b=0]

|`ELLIPSE_CONIC_TYPE`
|stem:[b^2-ac<0]

|`PARABOLA_CONIC_TYPE`
|stem:[b^2-ac=0]

|`HYPERBOLA_CONIC_TYPE`
|stem:[b^2-ac>0]

|`RECTANGULAR_HYPERBOLA_CONIC_TYPE`
|stem:[b^2-ac>0], and additionally stem:[a+c=0]
|===

This matrix form, its expansion into stem:[ax^2+bxy+cy^2+dx+ey+f=0], and the discriminant-based
classification above are worked out from first principles in
xref:reference.adoc#bib-phd-report[Irurueta's PhD report], &#167;1.2.5 "Conics" (eq. 1.24-1.27 and table 1.1,
printed pp. 6-8 / xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=18[PDF pp. 18-20]).

`Circle` and `Ellipse` are **independent, parametric classes** (center+radius; center+semi-axes+rotation) --
they do not extend `Conic`. They convert via `toConic()` / `setFromConic(Conic)`, and `setFromConic` checks
the conic type above (`Circle` requires `CIRCLE_CONIC_TYPE`; `Ellipse` accepts `ELLIPSE_CONIC_TYPE` or
`CIRCLE_CONIC_TYPE`, since every circle is also an ellipse).

== Quadrics

image::quadrics.svg[Sphere and ellipsoid as specializations of the general quadric surface]

`BaseQuadric` is the 3D analogue: a 4&#215;4 symmetric matrix with 10 parameters
stem:[(a,b,c,d,e,f,g,h,i,j)], for the surface
stem:[ax^2+by^2+cz^2+2dxy+2exz+2fyz+2gxw+2hyw+2izw+jw^2=0]. `Quadric`
tests point incidence and exposes `getTangentPlaneAt(point)` (derived in
<<tangent-lines-and-planes>>). The 4&#215;4 quadratic-form matrix, its 9-parameter expansion, and the
non-degenerate/degenerate standard-form tables (ellipsoid/paraboloid/hyperboloid vs. cone/cylinder) are the
direct 3D analogue of the conic case, worked out in xref:reference.adoc#bib-phd-report[the PhD report],
&#167;1.3.5 "Quadrics" (eq. 1.75-1.91, tables 1.2-1.3, printed pp. 15-16 /
xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=27[PDF pp. 27-28]).

Unlike conics, **quadrics have no built-in type-classification enum** -- there is no `QuadricType` equivalent
to `ConicType`, and no generic ellipsoid/hyperboloid/paraboloid classifier.

`Sphere` (center + radius) and `Ellipsoid` (center + rotation + three independent semi-axes) are, again,
independent parametric classes rather than `Quadric` subclasses:

* `Sphere.setFromQuadric(Quadric)` requires `a==b==c`, `a&#8800;0`, `d=e=f=0`.
* `Ellipsoid.toQuadric()` builds a canonical diagonal quadric then applies an `EuclideanTransformation3D` for
  center and rotation; there is **no general `setFromQuadric`** for `Ellipsoid` -- only conversion from a
  `Sphere`. Recovering an arbitrary ellipsoid's parameters from a general `Quadric` is not implemented.
* `Ellipsoid.getSurface()` uses Knud Thomsen's well-known closed-form approximation (exponent stem:[p \approx 1.6075]), since the exact ellipsoid surface area has no elementary closed form.

`Quadric.intersectWith(Plane)` computes the `Conic` obtained by slicing a quadric with a plane, by substituting
the plane equation into the quadric equation -- the algebra is carried out step by step in inline comments in
the source.

== Duality: DualConic and DualQuadric

image::conic-dual.svg[A conic as a locus of points versus its dual conic as an envelope of tangent lines]

A `Conic`/`Quadric` is a **locus of points**: stem:[\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{C}\, \mathbf{x} = 0]. Its dual, `DualConic`/
`DualQuadric`, describes the *exact same shape* but as an **envelope of tangent lines** (2D) or
**tangent planes** (3D) instead: stem:[\mathbf{l}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{C}^{*}\, \mathbf{l} = 0] for a tangent line `l`, where
stem:[\mathbf{C}^{*} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}].

[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
|Class |Represents |Locus test

|`Conic`
|points on the curve
|`isLocus(Point2D)` -- stem:[\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{C}\, \mathbf{x} \approx 0]

|`DualConic`
|lines tangent to the curve
|`isLocus(Line2D)` -- is this `Line2D` tangent to the conic?

|`Quadric`
|points on the surface
|`isLocus(Point3D)` -- stem:[\mathbf{x}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{Q}\, \mathbf{x} \approx 0]

|`DualQuadric`
|planes tangent to the surface
|`isLocus(Plane)` -- is this `Plane` tangent to the quadric?
|===

Both pairs share the same base-class matrix representation (`BaseConic` for `Conic`/`DualConic`,
`BaseQuadric` for `Quadric`/`DualQuadric`). The rest of this section derives, rather than just asserts, the
three facts the library's API relies on: how the tangent line/plane at a locus point is computed, why the dual
is the plain matrix inverse, and why the transform rule for conics/quadrics has that particular
inverse-transpose form.

[#tangent-lines-and-planes]
=== Tangent lines and tangent planes

Let stem:[\mathbf{m}] be a locus point of conic stem:[\mathbf{C}] (so stem:[S(\mathbf{m})=\mathbf{m}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{m}=0]), and let a
line stem:[\mathbf{l}] through stem:[\mathbf{m}] meet the conic again at a second point stem:[\mathbf{m}']. Every point of
the line can be written stem:[\mathbf{m}'' = \mathbf{m} + \lambda \mathbf{m}']; substituting into stem:[S(\mathbf{m}'')=0] and expanding
with the bilinear form stem:[S(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}')=\mathbf{m}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{m}'] gives a quadratic in stem:[\lambda]:

[stem]
++++
S(\mathbf{m}) + 2\lambda\,S(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}') + \lambda^2 S(\mathbf{m}') = 0
++++

which always has (up to) two roots -- a line meets a conic in two points. This is exactly what the internal
TODO at `Conic.java:478` cites (see <<references>> for the resolved citation); the full derivation is
xref:reference.adoc#bib-phd-report[PHD], &#167;1.2.6.1 "Line-conic intersections", Proof 1.2, eq. 1.34-1.44
(printed pp. 8-9 / xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=20[PDF pp. 20-21]).

The two intersection points coincide -- the line is *tangent* -- exactly when that quadratic's discriminant
vanishes. Fixing stem:[\mathbf{m}] on the conic (stem:[S(\mathbf{m})=0]), the discriminant condition collapses to the
linear equation stem:[S(\mathbf{m},\mathbf{m}')=\mathbf{m}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{m}'=0], which forces stem:[\mathbf{m}'=\mathbf{m}]. Writing this as
stem:[\mathbf{l}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{m}' = \mathbf{m}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{m}' = 0] identifies the tangent line at stem:[\mathbf{m}], up to scale,
as:

stem:[\mathbf{l} = \mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{m}]

(xref:reference.adoc#bib-phd-report[PHD], &#167;1.2.6.2 "Line-conic tangency", eq. 1.46-1.51, printed
pp. 10-11 / xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=22[PDF pp. 22-23]; the identical result appears as
Result 2.7 in
xref:reference.adoc#bib-hartley-zisserman[Hartley & Zisserman], chapter 2, p. 31). `Conic.getTangentLineAt`/
`tangentLineAt` (`Conic.java:420-460`) implement precisely this product:

[source,java]
----
Line2D tangent = conic.getTangentLineAt(point); // l = C·m
----

The same argument, one dimension up, uses three coplanar points instead of two collinear ones (PHD
&#167;1.3.6.1 "Plane-quadric intersections", eq. 1.92-1.100, printed pp. 17-18 /
xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=29[PDF pp. 29-30] -- it is
literally the same proof technique applied to a plane cutting a quadric instead of a line cutting a conic) and
gives the tangent plane at a locus point stem:[\mathbf{M}] as:

stem:[\boldsymbol{\Pi} = \mathbf{Q}\,\mathbf{M}]

implemented by `Quadric.getTangentPlaneAt`/`tangentPlaneAt` (`Quadric.java:516-560`):

[source,java]
----
Plane tangentPlane = quadric.getTangentPlaneAt(point); // Π = Q·M
----

=== The dual is the inverse: C&#42; = C&#8315;&#185; and Q&#42; = Q&#8315;&#185;

Insert stem:[\mathbf{C}^{-1}\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{I}] into the conic equation:

[stem]
++++
\mathbf{m}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{C}\, \mathbf{m} = \mathbf{m}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{C}\, \mathbf{C}^{-1} \mathbf{C}\, \mathbf{m} = 0
++++

Since stem:[\mathbf{l}=\mathbf{C}\mathbf{m}] is the tangent line at stem:[\mathbf{m}] (previous section), stem:[\mathbf{m}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{C} = \mathbf{l}^{\mathsf T}], so the expression above is stem:[\mathbf{l}^{\mathsf T} \mathbf{C}^{-1} \mathbf{l} = 0] -- which is exactly the dual
conic's own defining equation stem:[\mathbf{l}^{\mathsf T}\mathbf{C}^{*}\mathbf{l}=0]. Matching the two forces, assuming stem:[\mathbf{C}]
is non-singular:

stem:[\mathbf{C}^{*} = \mathbf{C}^{-1}]

(xref:reference.adoc#bib-phd-report[PHD], &#167;1.2.6.3 "Relation between conic and dual conic", eq. 1.52-1.54,
printed p. 11 / xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=23[PDF p. 23]; the same derivation from the
tangent-line result is given in
xref:reference.adoc#bib-hartley-zisserman[Hartley & Zisserman], chapter 2, p. 31). `Conic.getDualConic`/
`dualConic` (`Conic.java:236-269`) and the mirror `DualConic.getConic`/`conic` (`DualConic.java:246-277`)
compute exactly this matrix inverse (symmetrizing the result against numerical round-off), throwing
`DualConicNotAvailableException`/`ConicNotAvailableException` when the matrix is singular:

[source,java]
----
DualConic dual = conic.getDualConic(); // C* = C⁻¹
----

The identical algebra, one dimension up, gives stem:[\mathbf{Q}^{*}=\mathbf{Q}^{-1}] for quadrics, implemented by
`Quadric.getDualQuadric`/`dualQuadric` (`Quadric.java:262-299`) and `DualQuadric.getQuadric`/`quadric`
(`DualQuadric.java:272-307`).

=== Transforming conics, quadrics, and their duals

Under a point homography stem:[\mathbf{m}'=\mathbf{H}\mathbf{m}], lines transform contragradiently, stem:[\mathbf{l}'=\mathbf{H}^{-\mathsf T}\mathbf{l}].
Substituting stem:[\mathbf{m}=\mathbf{H}^{-1}\mathbf{m}'] into stem:[S(\mathbf{m})=0] and demanding that the transformed conic
stem:[\mathbf{C}'] reproduce the same locus in the new coordinates forces (up to scale):

stem:[\mathbf{C}' = \mathbf{H}^{-\mathsf T}\,\mathbf{C}\,\mathbf{H}^{-1}]

and the parallel argument on lines gives the dual rule stem:[\mathbf{C}^{*\prime} = \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{C}^{*}\,\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf T}].
Substituting the first rule into a plain inversion shows that transformation and duality commute:

[stem]
++++
(\mathbf{C}')^{*} = \left(\mathbf{H}^{-\mathsf T}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{H}^{-1}\right)^{-1} = \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{C}^{-1}\,\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf T} = \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{C}^{*}\,\mathbf{H}^{\mathsf T} = (\mathbf{C}^{*})'
++++

(xref:reference.adoc#bib-phd-report[PHD], &#167;1.2.7 "Transforming conics and dual conics", eq. 1.55-1.63 and
Proof 1.3, printed pp. 11-12 / xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=23[PDF pp. 23-24]; the transform
rules alone also appear as Results 2.13-2.14 in
xref:reference.adoc#bib-hartley-zisserman[Hartley & Zisserman], chapter 2, pp. 36-37). The identical algebra
one dimension up gives stem:[\mathbf{Q}'=\mathbf{T}^{-\mathsf T}\mathbf{Q}\mathbf{T}^{-1}], stem:[\mathbf{Q}^{*\prime}=\mathbf{T}\,\mathbf{Q}^{*}\,\mathbf{T}^{\mathsf T}],
and the same duality-commutes identity for quadrics (PHD &#167;1.3.7, eq. 1.114-1.116, printed pp. 20-21 /
xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=32[PDF pp. 32-33]). xref:transformations.adoc[] gives the general
table of how every geometric entity in this
library (points, lines, planes, conics, quadrics, cameras) transforms under each transformation class it
implements -- this section only shows *why* the conic/quadric rule takes that particular inverse-transpose
form.

=== The absolute conic and the dual absolute quadric

image::absolute-conic-quadric.svg[The absolute conic and dual absolute quadric on the plane at infinity]

`Conic.createCanonicalAbsoluteConic()` / `DualConic.createCanonicalDualAbsoluteConic()` return the identity
conic `(1,0,1,0,0,1)`, i.e. stem:[\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\infty = \boldsymbol{\Omega}_\infty^{*} = \mathbf{I}_3]; and
`DualQuadric.createCanonicalDualAbsoluteQuadric()` returns stem:[\mathbf{Q}_\infty^{*} = \mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,0)].
There is no `Quadric.createCanonicalAbsoluteQuadric()` -- the (non-dual) absolute quadric is never directly
instantiated in the library, only its degenerate dual. These are exactly the canonical forms derived in
xref:reference.adoc#bib-phd-report[PHD], &#167;1.4.3.1-1.4.3.3 (eq. 1.137-1.169, printed pp. 28-34 /
xref:attachment$irurueta-phd-thesis.pdf#page=40[PDF pp. 40-46]): the absolute conic stem:[\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\infty] is the point conic where the (non-dual) absolute
quadric stem:[\mathbf{Q}_\infty] meets the plane at infinity, and in the metric stratum both it and its dual reduce
to the identity/stem:[\mathrm{diag}(1,1,1,0)] forms above precisely because that is the condition under
which the conic-based orthogonality test stem:[\mathbf{m}_1^{\mathsf T}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_\infty \mathbf{m}_2 = 0] matches the ordinary
Euclidean dot product (the same construction is cross-referenced in
xref:reference.adoc#bib-hartley-zisserman[Hartley & Zisserman], chapter 3, &#167;&#167;3.6-3.7, pp. 81-83).
*Which* transformation brings an arbitrary projective reconstruction's dual absolute quadric back to this
canonical diagonal form -- the stratification story of upgrading a projective or affine reconstruction to the
metric stratum -- is the subject of xref:transformations.adoc[]; this page only establishes what the canonical
target looks like and why, since `Conic`/`DualQuadric` are the classes that hold it (also used in camera
self-calibration, see xref:pinhole-camera.adoc[]).
